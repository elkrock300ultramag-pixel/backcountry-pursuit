<div class="article-meta">Backcountry Pursuit Field Guide • Updated 2026</div><figure class="inline-photo"><img loading="lazy" src="../assets/images/packout.webp" alt="Elk pack-out in mountain terrain"><figcaption>Original Backcountry Pursuit field photography.</figcaption></figure><p>Elk hunting gear should be built around a few jobs: keep you moving, keep you warm and dry, help you find elk, and give you a safe way to handle an animal after the shot.</p>
<h2>Pack & Carry System</h2><p>Use a pack that fits your torso and can stabilize a heavy load. A meat shelf or load sling is valuable when the hunt turns into a pack-out.</p>
<div class="checklist"><h3>Core Pack List</h3><ul><li>Hunting pack with rigid frame</li><li>Game bags</li><li>Headlamp plus spare batteries</li><li>Water and filtration</li><li>Rain shell and insulation layer</li><li>Knife and compact sharpener</li><li>Navigation and emergency communication</li><li>First-aid and repair kit</li></ul></div>
<h2>Optics</h2><p>For western hunting, binoculars should be accessible without removing your pack. A tripod can make even standard binoculars far more effective during long glassing sessions.</p>
<h2>Clothing</h2><p>Build layers instead of relying on one heavy garment. Manage sweat on climbs, then add insulation before you become chilled.</p>
<h2>Kill Kit</h2><p>Keep game bags, gloves, flagging or waypoint tools, knife, and cord together so the system is ready when needed.</p>
<div class="callout"><strong>Field principle:</strong> Pack for the weather and consequences of being out overnight, not only for the weather at the trailhead.</div>
