<h2>Plan for the Workload</h2><p>Backcountry food should be easy to carry, simple to prepare, and enough to support long days. Build meals around calories you will actually eat when you are tired.</p><h2>Keep Breakfast Fast</h2><p>Opening-light plans rarely leave time for a complicated breakfast. Oatmeal, bars, nut butter, tortillas, or other quick food can get you moving without delaying the hunt.</p><h2>Use Trail Food During the Day</h2><p>Small snacks are often easier than stopping for a full lunch. Mix salty, sweet, protein, and carbohydrate options so you do not burn out on one food.</p><h2>Dinner Is Recovery</h2><p>At night, prioritize a warm meal, fluids, salt, and enough calories to recover for the next day. Keep a backup meal in camp for delays or an unexpected extra night.</p>
