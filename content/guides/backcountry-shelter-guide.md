<h2>Choose Shelter for the Conditions</h2><p>A shelter should match weather, exposure, trip length, and how mobile you need to be. A light tarp may be perfect in dry country while exposed alpine terrain may justify a stronger tent.</p><h2>Site Selection Matters</h2><p>Good shelter starts before the tent goes up. Avoid drainage bottoms, dead trees, obvious wind tunnels, avalanche paths, and low areas that collect cold air or water.</p><h2>Control Condensation</h2><p>Ventilation matters even when temperatures drop. Keep wet clothing out of the sleeping area when possible and use vents so moisture does not soak insulation from the inside.</p><h2>Practice Before the Trip</h2><p>Set the shelter up at home, in wind if possible, and know where every stake, pole, and guyline belongs before you need to pitch it in the dark.</p>
