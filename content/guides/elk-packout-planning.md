<div class="article-meta">Backcountry Pursuit Field Guide • 2026</div><figure class="inline-photo"><img loading="lazy" src="../assets/images/packout-meat.webp" alt="Plan the Elk Pack-Out Before the Shot"><figcaption>Original Backcountry Pursuit field photography.</figcaption></figure><p>The pack-out should be part of the hunt plan before you ever release an arrow or pull a trigger. Distance on a map can become a very different problem when it includes steep sidehills, deadfall, creek crossings, snow, darkness, and several heavy trips.</p><h2>Know the Real Distance</h2><p>Think about the route from likely kill locations back to a trail, road, camp, or vehicle—not just the walk in.</p><h2>Have a Meat-Care Plan</h2><p>Game bags, airflow, shade, cool ground, and realistic trip timing matter. Warm weather can turn a normal pack-out into a race against temperature.</p><h2>Build Loads You Can Control</h2><p>Stable, repeatable loads often beat one oversized load that causes a fall or injury.</p><h2>Use Help When It Makes Sense</h2><p>Partners, horses, llamas, or a prearranged pack service can dramatically change what terrain is realistic.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Field rule:</strong> If the pack-out plan only works when everything goes perfectly, it is not a good plan.</div>
