<div class="article-meta">Backcountry Pursuit Field Guide • Updated 2026</div><figure class="inline-photo"><img loading="lazy" src="../assets/images/horse-packout.webp" alt="Horse pack-out in western country"><figcaption>Original Backcountry Pursuit field photography.</figcaption></figure><p>A good elk camp is not the camp with the most gear. It is the one that keeps you dry, lets you sleep, organizes essentials, and is easy enough to move when the elk move.</p>
<h2>Shelter</h2><p>Choose shelter based on season, exposure, bug pressure, and whether you value minimum weight or more weather protection.</p>
<h2>Sleep System</h2><p>Do not overlook the sleeping pad. Ground insulation is a major part of staying warm, especially during clear mountain nights.</p>
<h2>Water</h2><p>Know your water plan before committing to camp. Carrying several days of water can erase the weight savings from an ultralight setup.</p>
<h2>Food</h2><p>Prioritize calorie density, easy preparation, and foods you will actually eat when tired. Keep one fast meal available for late returns.</p>
<h2>Camp Location</h2><p>Avoid camping directly on the elk movement you intend to hunt. A slightly longer walk can preserve the area around feeding and bedding cover.</p>
