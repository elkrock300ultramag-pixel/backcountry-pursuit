<figure class="inline-photo"><img src="../assets/images/aspen-elk.webp" alt="Build an Elk Calling Plan"><figcaption>Backcountry Pursuit field photography.</figcaption></figure><p>Calling elk is less about making more noise and more about giving a bull a believable reason to move. Before calling, use wind, terrain, cover and what the elk are already doing to decide whether the setup favors a response.</p><h2>Start With the Situation</h2><p>A herd bull with cows, a satellite bull and a quiet bull moving toward bedding cover can require different approaches. Listen first.</p><h2>Use Terrain to Finish the Setup</h2><p>Call from a position where an approaching bull has to enter shooting range before he can see the source. Ridges, timber pockets and small terrain breaks can help prevent a bull from hanging up.</p><h2>Know When to Stay Quiet</h2><p>If you have the wind and can move into position without being detected, sometimes the best calling decision is no call at all.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Field principle:</strong> Call to create an opportunity—not simply to get an answer.</div>
