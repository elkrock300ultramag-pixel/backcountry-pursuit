<h2>Build for a Clean Shot</h2><p>An elk setup should put reliability and penetration ahead of speed alone. Use equipment you can shoot accurately under pressure and from imperfect field positions.</p><h2>Arrow and Broadhead</h2><p>Choose a durable arrow with enough total weight and front-end strength for elk. Broadheads should be sharp, spin true, and group with your field points before the hunt.</p><h2>Know Your Real Range</h2><p>Your effective range is the distance where you can repeatedly make a clean shot from hunting positions, not the farthest distance you can hit a target on a calm range.</p><h2>Tune the Whole System</h2><p>Confirm cam timing, rest position, nock travel, broadhead flight, and sight marks before the season. Small tuning problems become large misses when adrenaline is high.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Rule:</strong> Confidence comes from a tuned bow and repeatable practice, not maximum speed.</div>
