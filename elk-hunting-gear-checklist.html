ELK GEAR

Elk Hunting Gear Checklist

A practical checklist for day hunts and backcountry elk camps without packing half the garage.

Backcountry Pursuit Field Guide • Updated 2026

Elk hunting gear should be built around a few jobs: keep you moving, keep you warm and dry, help you find elk, and give you a safe way to handle an animal after the shot.

Pack & Carry System

Use a pack that fits your torso and can stabilize a heavy load. A meat shelf or load sling is valuable when the hunt turns into a pack-out.

Core Pack List

  • Hunting pack with rigid frame
  • Game bags
  • Headlamp plus spare batteries
  • Water and filtration
  • Rain shell and insulation layer
  • Knife and compact sharpener
  • Navigation and emergency communication
  • First-aid and repair kit

Optics

For western hunting, binoculars should be accessible without removing your pack. A tripod can make even standard binoculars far more effective during long glassing sessions.

Clothing

Build layers instead of relying on one heavy garment. Manage sweat on climbs, then add insulation before you become chilled.

Kill Kit

Keep game bags, gloves, flagging or waypoint tools, knife, and cord together so the system is ready when needed.

Field principle: Pack for the weather and consequences of being out overnight, not only for the weather at the trailhead.
