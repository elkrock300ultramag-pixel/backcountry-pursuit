HUNTING FIELD GUIDE

Build an Elk Calling Plan

Calling works better when it is part of a plan instead of a nonstop sequence of bugles.

Calling elk is less about making more noise and more about giving a bull a believable reason to move. Before calling, use wind, terrain, cover and what the elk are already doing to decide whether the setup favors a response.

Start With the Situation

A herd bull with cows, a satellite bull and a quiet bull moving toward bedding cover can require different approaches. Listen first.

Use Terrain to Finish the Setup

Call from a position where an approaching bull has to enter shooting range before he can see the source. Ridges, timber pockets and small terrain breaks can help prevent a bull from hanging up.

Know When to Stay Quiet

If you have the wind and can move into position without being detected, sometimes the best calling decision is no call at all.

Field principle: Call to create an opportunity—not simply to get an answer.
