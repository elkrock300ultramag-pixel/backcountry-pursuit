CAMPING FIELD GUIDE

Backcountry Camp Food That Works

A simple way to plan high-calorie, low-fuss camp meals that are easy to prepare and still worth eating after a long day.

Plan for the Workload

Backcountry food should be easy to carry, simple to prepare, and enough to support long days. Build meals around calories you will actually eat when you are tired.

Keep Breakfast Fast

Opening-light plans rarely leave time for a complicated breakfast. Oatmeal, bars, nut butter, tortillas, or other quick food can get you moving without delaying the hunt.

Use Trail Food During the Day

Small snacks are often easier than stopping for a full lunch. Mix salty, sweet, protein, and carbohydrate options so you do not burn out on one food.

Dinner Is Recovery

At night, prioritize a warm meal, fluids, salt, and enough calories to recover for the next day. Keep a backup meal in camp for delays or an unexpected extra night.
