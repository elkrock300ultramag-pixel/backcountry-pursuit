Plan the Elk Pack-Out Before the Shot

A practical approach to distance, terrain, meat care, load hauling, and avoiding a bad situation after a successful elk hunt.

Backcountry Pursuit Field Guide • 2026

The pack-out should be part of the hunt plan before you ever release an arrow or pull a trigger. Distance on a map can become a very different problem when it includes steep sidehills, deadfall, creek crossings, snow, darkness, and several heavy trips.

Know the Real Distance

Think about the route from likely kill locations back to a trail, road, camp, or vehicle—not just the walk in.

Have a Meat-Care Plan

Game bags, airflow, shade, cool ground, and realistic trip timing matter. Warm weather can turn a normal pack-out into a race against temperature.

Build Loads You Can Control

Stable, repeatable loads often beat one oversized load that causes a fall or injury.

Use Help When It Makes Sense

Partners, horses, llamas, or a prearranged pack service can dramatically change what terrain is realistic.

Field rule: If the pack-out plan only works when everything goes perfectly, it is not a good plan.
